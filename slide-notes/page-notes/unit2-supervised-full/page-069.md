## Diagram Type
The main visual is a **Table**, specifically a **Confusion Matrix**. It is classified as such because it cross-tabulates the "Actual" ground truth classes against the "Predicted" classes generated by a machine learning model.

## Diagram / Visual Explanation
The table represents a binary classification outcome for 200 total patients.
*   **Rows (Actual):** Represent the true state of the patient.
    *   **Positive Row:** Patients who actually have COVID-19.
    *   **Negative Row:** Patients who actually do not have COVID-19.
*   **Columns (Predicted):** Represent the AI's prediction.
    *   **Positive Column:** Patients the AI flagged as having COVID-19.
    *   **Negative Column:** Patients the AI flagged as healthy.
*   **Cell Intersections:**
    *   **Actual Positive / Predicted Positive (90):** These are **True Positives (TP)**. The AI correctly identified 90 sick people.
    *   **Actual Positive / Predicted Negative (10):** These are **False Negatives (FN)**. The AI missed 10 sick people (Type II Error).
    *   **Actual Negative / Predicted Positive (15):** These are **False Positives (FP)**. The AI incorrectly flagged 15 healthy people as sick (Type I Error).
    *   **Actual Negative / Predicted Negative (85):** These are **True Negatives (TN)**. The AI correctly identified 85 healthy people.

## Math / Formula / Curve Notes
While no explicit formulas are written on the slide, the data provided allows for the following calculations:
*   **Total Samples (N):** $90 + 10 + 15 + 85 = 200$
*   **Accuracy:** $\frac{TP + TN}{Total} = \frac{90 + 85}{200} = \frac{175}{200} = 0.875$ or **87.5%**
*   **Precision (Positive Predictive Value):** $\frac{TP}{TP + FP} = \frac{90}{90 + 15} = \frac{90}{105} \approx 0.857$ or **85.7%**
*   **Recall (Sensitivity):** $\frac{TP}{TP + FN} = \frac{90}{90 + 10} = \frac{90}{100} = 0.90$ or **90%**
*   **Specificity:** $\frac{TN}{TN + FP} = \frac{85}{85 + 15} = \frac{85}{100} = 0.85$ or **85%**

## Concept Explanation
The concept being taught is the **Confusion Matrix** for binary classification. 
In machine learning, accuracy alone can be misleading, especially if classes are imbalanced. A confusion matrix breaks down the errors into two types:
1.  **Type I Error (False Positive):** Telling a healthy person they are sick. This might lead to unnecessary stress or further testing.
2.  **Type II Error (False Negative):** Telling a sick person they are healthy. In disease detection, this is often the most dangerous error because the patient will not receive treatment and may spread the disease to others.

By looking at this matrix, a developer can decide if the model needs to be tuned to be more "sensitive" (reducing False Negatives) even if it becomes less "specific" (increasing False Positives).
