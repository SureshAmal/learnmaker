## Concept Explanation
*   **Decision Tree:** A supervised learning algorithm used for both classification (predicting a category) and regression (predicting a continuous value). It functions by recursively splitting the dataset into smaller subsets based on the values of input features, creating a tree-like structure of nodes and branches.
*   **Predictor Variables:** These are the independent variables or "features" (e.g., age, income, temperature) used by the model to make a prediction.
*   **Response Variable:** This is the dependent variable or "target" (e.g., whether a customer will buy a product, the price of a house) that the model is trying to estimate.
*   **Non-linear Relationship:** In many real-world scenarios, the relationship between inputs and outputs is not a simple straight line. Decision trees are powerful because they don't assume a specific functional form (like a line or a plane). Instead, they partition the data space into distinct regions, allowing them to capture complex, non-linear patterns that models like Linear Regression might miss.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Definition:** A decision tree is a machine learning model used to map observations about an item to conclusions about the item's target value.
*   **Key Advantage:** Its primary strength is the ability to model **non-linear relationships** between predictor and response variables.
*   **Terminology:** Be prepared to define "predictor variables" (inputs) and "response variables" (outputs/targets).
*   **Application:** When would you choose a decision tree over a linear model? Answer: When the data suggests that the relationship between features and the target is complex and non-linear.
