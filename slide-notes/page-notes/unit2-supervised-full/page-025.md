## Concept Explanation
The slide discusses the characteristics of Decision Trees:

1.  **Interpretability (Easy to understand and visualize):** Decision trees mimic human decision-making. The model can be plotted as a tree structure where each node represents a question, making it "White Box" AI—users can see exactly why a specific prediction was made.
2.  **Data Versatility (Handles numerical and categorical data):** Unlike many algorithms (like Linear Regression or SVMs) that require categorical data to be converted into numbers (one-hot encoding), decision trees can naturally split data based on categories (e.g., "Color is Red") or numerical thresholds (e.g., "Age > 30").
3.  **Overfitting (Prone to over fitting):** This is the primary weakness. A decision tree can continue to split until every single training example is perfectly classified. This creates a highly complex tree that "memorizes" noise and outliers in the training data, leading to poor performance on new, unseen data.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Why are Decision Trees considered "interpretable"?** Because they can be visualized as a flowchart, allowing humans to trace the logic of a prediction.
*   **What types of data can Decision Trees process?** They are versatile and can handle both continuous (numerical) and discrete (categorical) features without extensive preprocessing.
*   **What is the main disadvantage of a deep Decision Tree?** Overfitting. The model becomes too specific to the training set and loses its ability to generalize.
*   **How do you combat overfitting in Decision Trees?** (Contextual knowledge) Through techniques like **pruning** (removing branches that provide little power) or setting a maximum depth for the tree.
