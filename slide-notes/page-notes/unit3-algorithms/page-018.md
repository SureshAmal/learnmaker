## Concept Explanation
This slide introduces **Ensemble Methods**, a powerful paradigm in machine learning.
*   **Ensemble Methods:** Instead of relying on a single "best" model, ensemble methods combine the predictions of multiple models (often called "base learners" or "weak learners") to produce a single, more accurate, and robust "strong learner."
*   **Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating):** This technique reduces variance. It involves training multiple versions of a model (like decision trees) on different random subsets of the training data (sampled with replacement). The final prediction is typically an average (for regression) or a majority vote (for classification).
*   **Boosting:** This technique primarily reduces bias. It trains models sequentially. Each subsequent model attempts to correct the errors made by the previous models in the sequence by assigning higher weights to the misclassified instances.
*   **Random Forests:** A specific and highly popular application of bagging. It builds an ensemble of decision trees. To ensure the trees are diverse (decorrelated), it introduces randomness not just in the data sampling, but also by selecting a random subset of features at every split in each tree.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Definition:** What are ensemble methods? (Combining multiple models to improve overall performance).
*   **Core Techniques:** Name the three main ensemble techniques mentioned on this slide. (Bagging, Boosting, Random Forests).
*   **Goal:** What is the primary advantage of using an ensemble over a single model? (Higher accuracy, better generalization, and reduced risk of overfitting).
*   **Bagging vs. Boosting:** What is the fundamental difference in how they are trained? (Bagging models are trained in parallel/independently; Boosting models are trained sequentially).
*   **Random Forest Context:** Which broader ensemble category does Random Forest belong to? (It is an extension of Bagging).
