## Concept Explanation
This slide introduces three fundamental concepts in machine learning theory:

1.  **Bias–Variance Tradeoff:** This is a central problem in supervised learning. **Bias** refers to error from erroneous assumptions in the learning algorithm (leading to underfitting). **Variance** refers to error from sensitivity to small fluctuations in the training set (leading to overfitting). The goal is to find a model complexity that minimizes the sum of both.
2.  **Model Capacity vs. Dataset Size:** **Capacity** refers to the range of functions a model can fit. A high-capacity model (like a deep neural network) can fit complex data but requires a large **Dataset Size** to generalize well. If capacity is too high for a small dataset, the model will likely overfit.
3.  **Occam’s Razor Principle in ML:** This philosophical principle states that "entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity." In machine learning, it suggests that among models with similar explanatory power (training performance), the simplest model is likely to generalize best to new data.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **What is the main theme of Section 6?** The relationship between how complex a model is and how well it generalizes to new data.
*   **Define Generalization:** The ability of a machine learning model to provide accurate predictions on new, previously unseen data, rather than just "memorizing" the training set.
*   **What are the three sub-topics covered here?** Bias-variance tradeoff, the relationship between model capacity and data volume, and the application of Occam's Razor.
*   **Why is Occam's Razor important in ML?** It justifies the use of regularization and simpler models to prevent overfitting when multiple models perform similarly on training data.
