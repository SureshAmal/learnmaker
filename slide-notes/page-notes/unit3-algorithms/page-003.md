## Concept Explanation
The slide introduces three pillars of model assessment:
*   **Importance of Evaluation:** In machine learning, a model's success isn't just about fitting the data it was trained on; it's about how it performs on new, unseen data. Evaluation provides the metrics to judge if a model is ready for deployment.
*   **Training vs. Real-world Performance:** This highlights the "generalization gap." A model might achieve 99% accuracy on training data (memorization) but perform poorly in the real world (generalization). Understanding this difference is key to identifying overfitting.
*   **Bias-Variance Tradeoff:** This is a core theoretical concept. 
    *   **Bias** is the error introduced by approximating a real-life problem with a simplified model (leading to underfitting). 
    *   **Variance** is the error introduced by the model's sensitivity to small fluctuations in the training set (leading to overfitting). 
    *   The "tradeoff" involves finding the sweet spot that minimizes the total error.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Why can't we rely solely on training accuracy?** Because training accuracy doesn't account for how the model will handle new data; it only shows how well the model learned the specific examples it was given.
*   **What is the primary goal of model evaluation?** To estimate the model's generalization performance on unseen data.
*   **Define the Bias-Variance Tradeoff.** It is the conflict in trying to simultaneously minimize two sources of error (bias and variance) that prevent supervised learning algorithms from generalizing beyond their training set.
*   **What does high variance usually indicate?** It usually indicates that the model is overfitting the training data, capturing noise as if it were a significant pattern.
