## Concept Explanation
The slide explains the core mechanism of **Linear Regression**:
1.  **Definition of Best Fit Line:** In a dataset where you have an input (independent variable) and an output (dependent variable), the best-fit line is the single straight line that most accurately captures the trend of the data points.
2.  **The Objective (Optimization):** Finding this line isn't random. The "best" line is mathematically defined as the one that has the smallest total "error." 
3.  **Error/Residuals:** The "difference" mentioned refers to the vertical distance between an actual observed data point and the point predicted by the line for that same input value. In machine learning, we use algorithms (like Gradient Descent) or mathematical methods (like Ordinary Least Squares) to find the line parameters (slope and intercept) that minimize these differences, often by minimizing the Sum of Squared Errors (SSE).

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **What is a best-fit line?** It is a straight line in linear regression that best represents the relationship between independent and dependent variables.
*   **What are the two types of variables in this context?** The independent variable (the input/feature) and the dependent variable (the output/target).
*   **What is the primary goal when calculating a best-fit line?** To minimize the difference (error/residual) between the actual observed data points and the values predicted by the model.
*   **How is "difference" typically measured?** While not on the slide, a student should know this refers to residuals, and the goal is often to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE).
