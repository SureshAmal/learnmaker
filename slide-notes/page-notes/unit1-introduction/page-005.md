## Concept Explanation
The slide covers three foundational pillars of ML in finance:

1.  **Fraud Detection:** Traditional systems relied on static rules (e.g., "flag if over $5,000"). ML improves this by learning the "normal" behavior of individual users. It identifies anomalies—such as a purchase in a different country or a sudden change in spending habits—in real-time to prevent unauthorized transactions.
2.  **Credit Scoring:** When a person applies for a loan, banks must decide if they are likely to pay it back. ML algorithms analyze historical data (income, debt, payment history, and sometimes non-traditional data) to generate a risk score. This allows for faster and often more accurate lending decisions compared to manual reviews.
3.  **Algorithmic Trading:** Financial markets move faster than humans can react. ML models are trained on massive datasets of historical stock prices, news sentiment, and economic indicators to identify patterns. These models then execute trades automatically at high speeds to capitalize on small market fluctuations.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Identify three financial applications of ML:** Fraud detection, Credit scoring, and Algorithmic trading.
*   **Explain Fraud Detection in ML:** It involves using anomaly detection techniques to identify transaction patterns that deviate from a user's historical baseline.
*   **Explain Credit Scoring in ML:** It is a predictive modeling task where the algorithm calculates the probability of default based on an applicant's features.
*   **Explain Algorithmic Trading:** It involves using time-series analysis and predictive modeling to automate buy/sell decisions in the stock market.
*   **Why is ML preferred over rule-based systems in finance?** ML can handle high-dimensional data, adapt to changing patterns (like new types of fraud), and process information at a speed and scale impossible for humans.
