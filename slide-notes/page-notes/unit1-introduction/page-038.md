## Concept Explanation
**Model Training** is the "learning" phase of machine learning. 
*   **Learning from Data:** The model is fed historical data (inputs and known outputs). It analyzes this data to find underlying mathematical relationships, such as "if feature X increases, feature Y usually decreases."
*   **Iterative Process:** Training is not a single step. The model makes a prediction, calculates how far off it was (the error), and then uses that information to slightly adjust its internal settings (parameters). This cycle repeats many times (often called "epochs") until the error is as low as possible.
*   **Optimization:** This involves using mathematical algorithms (like Gradient Descent) to find the absolute best parameters that result in the highest accuracy or lowest loss.
*   **Validation & Generalization:** The ultimate goal isn't just to get the right answers for the data the model has already seen, but to ensure it works on **unseen data**. Validation techniques help ensure the model hasn't just "memorized" the training set (overfitting) but has actually learned general rules.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Definition:** Model training is the stage where an algorithm is exposed to historical data to learn patterns and dependencies.
*   **The Three Pillars of Training:**
    1.  **Iteration:** The repetitive cycle of adjusting parameters to reduce error.
    2.  **Optimization:** The process of fine-tuning the model for peak predictive performance.
    3.  **Validation:** Ensuring the model's accuracy extends to new, unseen datasets (generalization).
*   **Goal:** The primary objective is to minimize the error function and enhance the model's predictive accuracy.
