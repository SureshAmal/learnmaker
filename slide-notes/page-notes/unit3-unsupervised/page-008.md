## Concept Explanation
The slide introduces three fundamental pillars of machine learning theory:
1.  **Bias-Variance Tradeoff:** This is the conflict between trying to minimize two sources of error that prevent supervised learning algorithms from generalizing beyond their training set. **Bias** is error from overly simple assumptions (leading to underfitting), while **Variance** is error from excessive sensitivity to small fluctuations in the training data (leading to overfitting).
2.  **Model Capacity vs. Dataset Size:** "Capacity" refers to the range of functions a model can fit. A high-capacity model (like a deep neural network) can learn complex patterns but requires a large dataset to avoid memorizing noise. This section likely explores how increasing data allows for more complex models.
3.  **Occam’s Razor Principle:** A philosophical rule stating that "entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity." In ML, this means that if two models perform similarly, the simpler one (with fewer parameters or simpler structure) is generally preferred because it is more likely to generalize well to new data.
