## Math / Formula / Curve Notes
*   **$Y = wx + b$**: This is the standard equation for a straight line.
    *   **$Y$**: The predicted output or dependent variable.
    *   **$w$**: The weight (slope), determining the steepness of the line.
    *   **$x$**: The input feature or independent variable.
    *   **$b$**: The bias (y-intercept), determining where the line crosses the vertical axis.
*   **$h_i(x)$**: Represents a specific hypothesis function.
    *   In $h_1(x)$, $w=2$ and $b=1$.
    *   In $h_2(x)$, $w=3$ and $b=5$.
    *   In $h_3(x)$, $w=5$ and $b=-2$.
*   **$H = \{2x + 1, 3x + 5, 5x - 2, \dots\}$**: This defines the **Hypothesis Space** $H$ as the set of all functions the learning algorithm is allowed to consider. In this context, $H$ is the set of all linear functions of one variable.

## Concept Explanation
*   **Linear Hypothesis:** In machine learning, we often start with an assumption about the shape of the data's underlying pattern. A linear hypothesis assumes that the relationship between input and output is a straight line.
*   **Parameters ($w, b$):** These are the learnable components of the model. The goal of training a machine learning model is to find the specific values for $w$ and $b$ that make the hypothesis $h(x)$ best fit the training data.
*   **Hypothesis Space ($H$):** This is the "search space" for the algorithm. By choosing a linear model, we restrict the algorithm to only look at straight lines. The hypothesis space is the collection of every possible line that the model could potentially represent. The learning process is essentially searching through this space $H$ to find the single best hypothesis $h$.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Define a linear hypothesis:** It is represented by the equation $h(x) = wx + b$.
*   **What are $w$ and $b$?** $w$ is the weight (slope) and $b$ is the bias (intercept). They are the parameters the model learns.
*   **What is a Hypothesis Space ($H$)?** It is the set of all candidate functions (hypotheses) that a learning algorithm considers during the training process.
*   **How do parameters relate to the Hypothesis Space?** Every unique combination of parameters ($w, b$) corresponds to one specific hypothesis $h$ within the larger hypothesis space $H$.
*   **Significance of $H$:** The choice of hypothesis space defines the model's bias. A linear hypothesis space cannot accurately model non-linear data patterns.
