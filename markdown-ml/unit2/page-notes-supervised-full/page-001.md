## Concept Explanation
The slide introduces the foundational pillars of **Supervised Learning**, a branch of machine learning where models are trained on labeled datasets.
*   **Regression:** A technique used to predict continuous numerical values (e.g., predicting the price of a house based on its features).
*   **Cost Function:** A mathematical formula that measures the error between the model's predictions and the actual target values. The goal of training is to minimize this value.
*   **Gradient Descent:** An optimization algorithm used to iteratively update model parameters to find the minimum of the cost function.
*   **Bias-Variance Tradeoff:** A fundamental concept describing the tension between a model's ability to capture the underlying pattern (low bias) and its sensitivity to noise in the training data (low variance).
*   **Overfitting:** Occurs when a model is too complex and learns the noise in the training data, leading to poor performance on new, unseen data.
*   **Underfitting:** Occurs when a model is too simple to learn the underlying structure of the data, resulting in poor performance on both training and test sets.
*   **Regularization:** A set of techniques (like L1 or L2 regularization) used to prevent overfitting by adding a penalty term to the cost function, discouraging overly complex models.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Define Supervised Learning:** It is a type of machine learning where the algorithm learns from a labeled dataset, mapping inputs to known outputs.
*   **List the core topics of Unit II:** Regression, Cost Functions, Gradient Descent, Bias-Variance Tradeoff, Overfitting, Underfitting, and Regularization.
*   **What is the purpose of a Cost Function?** To quantify how "wrong" the model is, providing a metric that the optimization algorithm (like Gradient Descent) can minimize.
*   **Explain the difference between Overfitting and Underfitting:** Overfitting is high variance/low bias (too complex); Underfitting is low variance/high bias (too simple).
*   **What is the goal of Regularization?** To improve a model's generalization ability by preventing it from becoming overly complex and overfitting the training data.
