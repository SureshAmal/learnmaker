## Concept Explanation
The slide introduces three pillars of model assessment:
1.  **Importance of Evaluation:** In ML, building a model is only half the task. Evaluation is critical to determine if the model actually solves the problem it was designed for, to compare different algorithms, and to tune parameters for optimal results.
2.  **Training vs. Real-World Performance:** This highlights the concept of **Generalization**. A model might achieve 99% accuracy on the data it has already seen (training set) but perform poorly on new, unseen data (real-world). Understanding this gap is vital to avoid "overfitting," where a model memorizes noise instead of learning patterns.
3.  **Bias-Variance Tradeoff:** This is a central theoretical framework in ML.
    *   **Bias** is the error introduced by approximating a real-life problem (which may be complex) by a much simpler model. High bias leads to **underfitting**.
    *   **Variance** is the error introduced by the model's sensitivity to small fluctuations in the training set. High variance leads to **overfitting**.
    *   The "tradeoff" refers to the challenge of minimizing both to achieve the lowest possible total error.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Why is evaluation necessary?** To ensure the model generalizes well to new data and to provide a metric for comparing different models.
*   **What is the "Generalization Gap"?** It is the difference between the performance on the training data and the performance on unseen test/real-world data.
*   **Define Bias:** Error from overly simplistic assumptions in the learning algorithm (leads to underfitting).
*   **Define Variance:** Error from high sensitivity to specific noise in the training data (leads to overfitting).
*   **What is the goal of the Bias-Variance Tradeoff?** To find the "sweet spot" in model complexity that minimizes the total error (Bias² + Variance + Irreducible Noise).
