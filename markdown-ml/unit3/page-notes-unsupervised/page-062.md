## Concept Explanation
The slide introduces **Ensemble Methods**, a powerful paradigm in machine learning where multiple models (often called "base learners" or "weak learners") are combined to solve a single problem. The core idea is that a group of "weak" models can work together to form a "strong" learner that is more accurate and robust than any individual component.

*   **Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating):** A method that reduces variance by training multiple versions of a model independently on different random subsets of the training data (sampled with replacement). The final prediction is typically an average (for regression) or a majority vote (for classification).
*   **Boosting:** A sequential technique that reduces both bias and variance. It trains models one after another, where each subsequent model focuses on correcting the errors made by the previous ones by assigning higher weights to misclassified data points.
*   **Random Forests:** A specific application of bagging that uses decision trees. It adds further diversity by selecting a random subset of features at each split in the trees, which helps to decorrelate the individual trees and improve generalization.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **Definition:** What are ensemble methods? (Combining multiple models to improve overall performance).
*   **Primary Goal:** Why use ensembles? (To reduce overfitting/variance, reduce bias, and improve predictive accuracy).
*   **Key Techniques:** Be prepared to name and distinguish between Bagging, Boosting, and Random Forests.
*   **Bagging vs. Boosting:** Remember that Bagging builds models in **parallel** (independently), while Boosting builds them **sequentially** (dependently).
*   **Random Forest Uniqueness:** Understand that Random Forest is an ensemble of decision trees that uses both data bagging and feature bagging.
