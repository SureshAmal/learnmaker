## Concept Explanation
Structural (or Syntactic) Representation is a paradigm in pattern recognition that focuses on the **topology** of data rather than just its statistical features.

1.  **Shift in Focus:** In many machine learning tasks, we use "Feature Vectors" (lists of numbers like height, weight, or pixel intensity). However, structural representation is used when the *way things are put together* defines the object. For example, a chair is defined by the relationship between four legs, a seat, and a backrest, regardless of their exact numeric dimensions.
2.  **Graphs & Trees:** These are used to represent non-linear relationships. 
    *   **Nodes:** Represent the "primitives" or sub-parts of the object.
    *   **Edges:** Represent the physical or logical connection between those parts.
3.  **Strings & Grammars:** This approach treats a pattern like a language. 
    *   **Primitives:** The basic building blocks (like the alphabet).
    *   **Strings:** A sequence of these blocks.
    *   **Grammar:** The rules that dictate how these blocks can be legally combined to form a valid pattern.
4.  **Application:** The chemical molecule example is perfect because a molecule's properties are determined by its specific bonding structure (which atom is connected to which), not just the total count of atoms.

## Exam / Viva Points

*   **Key Tools:** Be ready to name **Graphs, Trees, Strings, and Grammars** as the primary methods for structural modeling.
