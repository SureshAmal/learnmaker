## Concept Explanation
The slide introduces the concept of **Generalization** in Machine Learning.
*   **Generalization:** This refers to a model's ability to make accurate predictions on new, previously unseen data. A model that generalizes well has learned the underlying patterns and relationships in the data rather than just memorizing specific examples.
*   **Training Data:** The dataset used to "teach" the model. While high accuracy here is good, it is not the final goal.
*   **Unseen/Test Data:** Data that the model has never encountered during the training phase. This acts as a "final exam" to see if the model truly understands the concepts or has simply memorized the training set.
*   **The Core Goal:** The ultimate aim is to avoid **overfitting** (where a model performs perfectly on training data but fails on new data) by creating a robust model that works reliably in real-world scenarios.

## Exam / Viva Points
*   **What is the primary goal of machine learning modeling?** The goal is to build a generalized model that performs well on unseen or test data.
*   **Define "Unseen Data":** It is data that was not used during the training process, used specifically to evaluate the model's predictive power.
*   **Why is performance on training data alone insufficient?** High performance on training data might indicate overfitting, where the model learns noise or specific details of the training set that do not apply to the general population.
*   **What does "Generalization" mean in the context of ML?** It is the ability of a model to apply what it has learned from the training set to new, independent data points.
